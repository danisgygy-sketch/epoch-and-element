## 1. Narration (verbatim)

**THE 2,000-YEAR-OLD COMPUTER**

> More than two thousand years ago, someone in ancient Greece built a machine to predict the sky.
>
> Made of bronze and packed with gears, it tracked the cycles of the Sun and Moon—and predicted eclipses.
>
> Found in a shipwreck near Antikythera in 1901, it became known as the Antikythera Mechanism.
>
> Today, 82 fragments survive, including 30 corroded bronze gears. Together, they reveal extraordinary engineering.
>
> But its full design is still debated. Some parts are lost, and researchers are still piecing together how it worked.
>
> Often called the world’s oldest known analogue computer.
>
> A small machine built to model an immense sky.

## 4. Timing

**Method.** No voice was recorded or synthesised, so this is a **model, not a measured reading**. Words are weighted by syllables and calibrated so the whole script runs at the stated words-per-minute; a documentary pause budget of **5.9 s** is added (shorter after commas, longest after the end of each block).

| Pace | Narration ends |
|---|---|

| **150 wpm (nominal)** | **46.7 s** |

**Video length.** Narration ends at about 46.7 s; add a 1.3 s tail for the brand mark to settle, giving a nominal **48.0 s = 1,440 frames**.

**Tolerance.** The brief allows up to **55 s (1,650 frames)**. Even at a slow 145 wpm the video would run about 49.4 s, so the cap leaves a margin of more than 5 s. The cap is **1,650 frames** (the earlier 45 s cap no longer applies).

## 6. Delivery direction

- **Tone:** calm documentary narrator, curious and quietly impressed; never theatrical, no trailer voice. Clear international English.
- **Pace:** 150 wpm nominal. The two hook lines are slightly slower; the closing line is the slowest of the script.
- **Numbers:** "1901" as *nineteen oh one*; "82" and "30" as plain, level numerals. They are on-screen labels too, so do not stress them heavily.
- **Pauses:** the longest pauses (0.6–0.7 s) follow "the sky.", "eclipses.", "Mechanism.", "engineering." and "how it worked.", so each beat lands before the next begins. Keep the short comma pauses short (0.2–0.25 s).
- **Inflection plan:** line 1 lifts slightly on "sky" (an open hook). "But its full design is still debated" drops in pitch on "debated" and stays honest and unhurried. "Often called…" is said a little lighter than the facts around it (it is a popular label, not a claim of proof). The last line is level and warm.
- **Pronunciation:** pick one pronunciation of "Antikythera" and keep it for the whole video (recommended *an-tih-kih-THEER-uh*; the Greek is closer to *an-dee-KEE-thee-rah*). The apostrophe in "world’s" is a possessive.

## 1. Final narration (v1.1)

> Two thousand years ago, someone built a computer from bronze gears.
>
> No electricity. No screen. Just a hand-crank—and a machine designed to model the sky.
>

>
> Turn the crank, and its dials could track the cycles of the Sun and Moon, follow complex calendars, and predict eclipses.
>
> It is known as the Antikythera Mechanism—the oldest known analogue computer.
>

Three wording notes for the approver, none of which change the script:

- **"hand-crank"** — the crank is an inference, not a surviving part (C15). Keep the line; the 3D crank must look reconstructed.
- **"oldest known analogue computer"** — the peer-reviewed phrase is "oldest known mechanical calculator" (C16). **Decided by the studio:** keep the popular label as the hook and state the scientific term on screen (see §2b).
- **"predict eclipses"** — correct, but it predicts *when eclipses were possible*, not visibility at a place (C11).

Each word is weighted by syllable count and calibrated so the whole script runs at the stated words-per-minute, then a documentary pause budget of **4.4 s** is added (shorter after commas, longer at the end of each sentence block).

## 5. Delivery direction

- **Tone:** a calm, curious documentary narrator. Informed and quietly impressed, never theatrical, no trailer voice.
- **Language:** clear international English, neutral accent. Consistent pronunciation of **Antikythera** throughout (recommended: *an-tih-kih-THEER-uh*; the Greek is closer to *an-dee-KEE-thee-rah*; pick one for the whole project).
- **"BCE"** is read as the letters B-C-E.
- **Pace:** 150–155 wpm. Slightly slower on the hook, slightly faster through the list in line 4.
- **Pauses:** the shortest pauses are after the commas in line 4, so the list reads as one run. The longest (0.5 s) belong to the two "reveal" moments: after "gears." and after "eclipses." The 0.3 s before "debated" lets the music breathe.
- **Do not:** smile-read the hook, ramp energy at the end, or stress "computer" as a gimmick. Let the word land flat and certain.
- **Inflection plan:** line 1 rises slightly on "gears" (a hook, an open question). Line 2 is level and plain. The caveat in line 6 drops in pitch on "debated" and resolves warmly on "could be".

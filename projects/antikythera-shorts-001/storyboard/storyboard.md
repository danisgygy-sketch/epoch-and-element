# Storyboard — The 2,000-Year-Old Computer (Script v2)

Format 9:16 · 1080 × 1920 · 30 fps · nominal **48.0 s = 1,440 frames** · hard cap **55.0 s = 1,650 frames**.
Timings come from the narration model in `script/voiceover-en.md` (150 wpm) and will shift once a real voice take exists. The previous storyboard (script v1.1) is kept in `storyboard/archive/`.

**Status:** the narration is the studio's, unchanged. **Every on-screen text and visual below is a proposal derived from that narration and needs approval.** They add no claim beyond the script and `research/fact-check.md`.

**B** = Blender 3D · **R** = Remotion 2D. Evidence tiers: A surviving · B inferred · C hypothesis · D dramatised (see `research/fact-check.md`).

## What changed from the first storyboard

The new script reorders the story, so the six scenes keep their roles but follow the narration:

| New scene | Narration | Role | Compared with the first plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 The Hook | "More than two thousand years ago… predict the sky." | Hook | Same role; no "computer" yet |
| 02 Calculating the Sky | "Made of bronze… predicted eclipses." | What it did | **Moved earlier** (was scene 05) |
| 03 Discovery | "Found in a shipwreck… Antikythera Mechanism." | Wreck + name | Moved later; now also names the object |
| 04 The Mechanism Revealed | "Today, 82 fragments survive… extraordinary engineering." | Evidence | Same role |
| 05 What We Don't Know | "But its full design is still debated… how it worked." | Uncertainty | **New**; shows the evidence/reconstruction boundary |
| 06 Final Reveal | "Often called… A small machine built to model an immense sky." | Label + close | Same role |

The "Not a Modern Computer" scene (*No electricity. No screen.*) is **gone** because the new script does not say it. Calendars are no longer narrated either, so the calendar-dial diagram is unused.

## Global rules

- **Vertical composition.** Important content sits in the centre band. Reserve the bottom **440 px** for YouTube's Shorts interface and **120 px** on the right for its icons. Captions live at y ≈ 1230–1470.
- **One idea per shot.** Every shot answers the question in the viewer's mind at that moment.
- **No archival look.** Scene 03 carries a small persistent **DRAMATIZED RECONSTRUCTION** label.
- **Evidence legend.** Tier C geometry is always ghosted in Scientific Cyan and labelled.
- **Motion.** Slow, deliberate camera. Gears turn at one constant, believable speed.
- **Edit rhythm.** Cut on the narration's sentence breaks; hold macro shots at least 1.2 s; no shot longer than 6 s.
- **Sound** is out of scope for now (the studio will handle it). Audio cues in earlier drafts are stale; see the banner in `audio/audio-direction.md`.

## Scene 01 — The Hook · 0.0–6.8 s · frames 0–205

**Narration:** "More than two thousand years ago, someone in ancient Greece built a machine to predict the sky."

| Beat | Time | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | 0.0–2.3 | **B.** Black. A low raking light picks out one bronze gear in extreme macro (85 mm, shallow DOF). It begins to turn slowly. |
| 1b | 2.3–4.0 | **B.** A second gear enters and **engages** precisely; slow 4% push-in. |
| 1c | 4.0–6.8 | **B.** The camera pulls back and up through the gear stack to reveal the dial plate; a faint ring of stars and zodiac marks glows on the dark navy ("predict the sky"). |

**On-screen text (R, proposed):** 0.0 s **MORE THAN 2,000 YEARS AGO…** (small, wide-tracked, upper third); 3.9 s **A MACHINE TO PREDICT THE SKY.**
**Feeling:** mysterious, intelligent, cinematic.
**Fact-check:** line 1 (C01, C22). The visual makes no claim about who built it or where.

| Beat | Time | Shot |

## Scene 03 — Discovery · 14.1–21.7 s · frames 423–650

**Narration:** "Found in a shipwreck near Antikythera in 1901, it became known as the Antikythera Mechanism."

| Beat | Time | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| 3a | 14.1–17.4 | **B.** Hard cut to **underwater**: a cold teal-blue column of light, drifting particles, a sandy seabed with amphora shards and timber ribs. Slow lateral dolly. |
| 3b | 17.4–21.7 | **B → R.** A corroded bronze lump half-buried among debris rises toward the light; the colour warms from teal to bronze as it nears the surface. |

**On-screen text (R, proposed):** 15.5 s **ANTIKYTHERA, GREECE — 1901** as a small locator; **DRAMATIZED RECONSTRUCTION** bottom-left from 14.1 s to 21.7 s. The name is spoken here and shown large in scene 06, so no title card now.
**Fact-check:** line 3 (C02, C03, C05); low-severity flag on "Found … in 1901" (see `fact-check.md`). Say **1901** only; no month. This is a **dramatisation**, not archival footage, and it does not show the real fragments.

## Scene 04 — The Mechanism Revealed · 21.7–30.5 s · frames 650–915

**Narration:** "Today, 82 fragments survive, including 30 corroded bronze gears. Together, they reveal extraordinary engineering."

| Beat | Time | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| 4a | 21.7–23.4 | **B.** Macro of a **corroded fragment** with gear teeth under crust (Tier A treatment: damage, patina, loss). |
| 4b | 23.4–25.9 | **B.** The fragments slide apart into an **exploded view**; hidden gears resolve in clean bronze, spaced in layers. |
| 4c | 25.9–30.5 | **B.** The exploded stack settles into the assembled mechanism; a slow glide across the layered gearing ("extraordinary engineering"). |

**On-screen text (R, proposed):** 21.7 s **82 SURVIVING FRAGMENTS** (count-up); 23.45 s **30 SURVIVING GEARS** (count-up). Optional: **ABOUT ONE THIRD SURVIVES** ring (D4) at 27 s (C08; not in the script, so it needs approval).
**Fact-check:** line 4–5 (C06, C07, C09, C17, C18). Use **only** 82 and 30; never 35, 37 or 69.

## Scene 05 — What We Don't Know · 30.5–39.1 s · frames 915–1172

**Narration:** "But its full design is still debated. Some parts are lost, and researchers are still piecing together how it worked."

| Beat | Time | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| 5a | 30.5–33.5 | **B.** The assembled mechanism holds; outer gear groups begin to **ghost out** into translucent Scientific Cyan wireframes (Tier C). |
| 5b | 33.5–34.8 | **B.** On "Some parts are lost": whole sections fade, leaving visible empty space around the solid surviving core. |
| 5c | 34.8–39.1 | **B.** The ghost gears reassemble slowly around the core, as if being worked out ("piecing together"). |

**On-screen text (R, proposed):** 31.0 s legend chips **SURVIVING EVIDENCE · RECONSTRUCTION** (second chip in Scientific Cyan).
**Fact-check:** line 6 (C08, C13, C14, C21). Ghost geometry is never shown in solid bronze.

## Scene 06 — Final Reveal · 39.1–48.0 s · frames 1172–1440

**Narration:** "Often called the world’s oldest known analogue computer. A small machine built to model an immense sky."

| Beat | Time | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| 6a | 39.1–43.6 | **B.** Slow, low orbit around the **completed artistic reconstruction in its case**: warm bronze highlights on dark navy. |
| 6b | 43.6–48.0 | **B.** Calm pull-back to a wide of the small machine against a vast, faintly starred sky ("immense sky"). The brand mark fades in over the last 2 s. |

**On-screen text (R, proposed):**
- 39.1 s **THE ANTIKYTHERA MECHANISM** (three lines).
- 41.2 s subtitle **THE OLDEST KNOWN ANALOGUE COMPUTER** (the popular label, as "oldest known" is spoken).
- 43.2 s **SCIENTIFIC TERM: MECHANICAL CALCULATOR** in smaller type beneath (the careful term; studio decision, see README; on screen only, no narration change). Held about 2.8 s (to 46.0 s).
- 46.0 s **EPOCH & ELEMENT** + **The Forces That Shaped Our World.** (held to the end).

**Fact-check:** lines 7–8 (C16, C19). Never "the first computer".

## 2. Description

```
Recovered from a shipwreck near the Greek island of Antikythera in 1901, this corroded bronze machine was probably built between 150 and 100 BCE. Its gears tracked the cycles of the Sun and Moon, followed calendars and predicted eclipses.

The Antikythera Mechanism: Ancient Greece's astronomical calculator.

What is true, and what is reconstructed
• 82 fragments and 30 gears survive, about one third of the original.
• "Analogue computer" is the popular label; the more careful scientific term is "mechanical calculator". It is not a digital computer.
• The crank, the case and any planetary gearing shown are reconstructions. Parts of the design remain debated.
• The shipwreck scene and all 3D visuals are artistic visualisations made for this video, not archival footage.
```

Format follows Production Bible §8.4. Evidence detail lives in `fact-check.md` (claim IDs in brackets) and `sources.md`.

```text
PROJECT: S001 — EPOCH & ELEMENT Shorts 001

WORKING TITLE: The 2,000-Year-Old Computer
(alternate title from the Bible: The Machine That Predicted Eclipses)

MAIN QUESTION:
How could people build something resembling an analogue computer
more than 2,000 years ago?

CORE ANSWER (as far as the evidence allows):
Ancient Greek makers combined knowledge of astronomical cycles with precise
bronze gearing to build a hand-driven mechanical astronomical calculator,
probably between 150 and 100 BCE. It modelled the Sun and Moon, kept calendars
and predicted eclipses. Only about a third survives, so its complete design,
its maker and some of its functions remain debated. The video says this
openly rather than presenting a full reconstruction as proven.

KEY FACTS:
- Recovered from a shipwreck near the Greek island of Antikythera; wreck found
  1900, mechanism recovered 1901, recognised as a geared device in 1902. [C02-C05]
- Preserved in 82 fragments bearing 30 surviving gears, in bronze. [C06, C07, C09]
- About one third of the original survives. [C08]
- Tracks Sun and Moon cycles and calendars; predicts lunar and solar eclipses
  (Saros-type cycle, Babylonian-derived arithmetic). [C10, C11]
- Inscriptions show it was also meant to display planetary positions; that
  machinery is lost. [C13]
- Researchers found the Moon's variable speed realised in the gearing
  (a pin-and-slot linkage, after Hipparchos' lunar theory). [C18]

IMPORTANT DATES / NUMBERS:
- Probably 150-100 BCE (never a single year). [C01]
- Wreck 1900; recovery 1901 (month disputed, not stated); recognised 1902.
- 82 fragments; 30 surviving gears; about one third survives.
- NOT to be used: 35, 37 or 69 gears (inferred or reconstructed), any
  tooth count, any single construction year, any month of recovery.

WHAT IS WELL ESTABLISHED:
- Location, the 1900-1901 recovery, bronze, 82 fragments, 30 surviving gears.
- Solar and lunar functions, calendars, eclipse prediction.
- It is a mechanical, analogue astronomical calculator, not a digital computer.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN OR DEBATED:
- Exact date of construction; maker; place of origin. [C01, C22]
- The hand crank (lost; inferred). [C15]
- The front planetary gearing: the 2021 model (34 reconstructed gears; 69 in
  the full model) is a peer-reviewed hypothesis, not a proven design,
  and the Dragon Hand for the lunar nodes has no direct physical evidence. [C14]
- The case dimensions (Ministry: "possible dimensions"). [C19]
- Terminology: "analogue computer" is the popular label; the peer-reviewed
  term is "mechanical calculator". The video uses the popular label as the
  hook and states the scientific term on screen (decision, see README). [C16]

VISUAL OPPORTUNITIES:
- Macro gear engagement; one crank turning every gear.
- Corroded fragment to exploded view (surviving evidence vs reconstruction).
- Sun/Moon, calendar and eclipse-cycle diagrams.
- Evidence legend: solid = surviving, Scientific Cyan ghost = reconstruction.

SOURCES:

- S2 Freeth et al., Nature 2006 (abstract only)
- S3 Seiradakis & Edmunds, Nature Astronomy 2018 (abstract only)
- S5 Freeth et al., Scientific Reports 2021 (full text)
- S4 UCL 2021 press release: NOT READ (HTTP 403); not cited

4. Dating debate (Jones 2020, ISAW Papers 17, not yet read).
```
